About the Programme

Grassroots cooperation, at a regional scale

Grants for Grassroots Initiatives (GGI) is the Western Balkans Fund's annual grant scheme for regional cooperation projects led by civil society. It is implemented under Phase III of the EU/WBF Joint Action, with co-funding from the European Union and from the six Contracting Parties.

The scheme is designed to empower CSOs, with a particular focus on grassroots and young, emerging organisations rooted in their communities, to take part in regional initiatives that contribute to good neighbourly relations, reconciliation, democratic governance and EU integration.

Each project must bring together partners from at least three different Contracting Parties. This is a deliberate design choice: it turns funding into a tool for region-building, not just bilateral exchange.

GGI supports three areas of intervention: cultural cooperation, education and scientific exchanges, and sustainable development. It ensures balanced geographical distribution of funds across the WB6.

Areas of Intervention

What GGI funds

GGI supports cross-border regional projects in three thematic areas.

Cultural Cooperation
Regional projects that build people-to-people links and good neighbourly relations through shared cultural heritage, intercultural dialogue and creative cooperation.
  • Awareness of regional cultural heritage: history, art, culinary, tourism
  • Intercultural dialogue and protection of archaeological sites & monuments
  • Joint work among museums, galleries, artistic ensembles, film producers
Education & Scientific Exchanges
Cooperation among schools, universities, research institutions and teachers across the region, sharing best practices and creating joint regional initiatives in education and research.
  • Joint educational & research initiatives across borders
  • Actions tackling brain drain and supporting Western Balkans researchers
  • STEM popularisation for women and youth; lifelong learning
Sustainable Development
Inclusive economic growth, environmental protection and climate action at the regional level, including innovation, tourism and cross-border cooperation among local authorities.
  • Inclusive & sustainable economic growth; start-up and innovation forums
  • Awareness on environmental protection and climate change
  • Cross-border local-authority cooperation for economic & social development
Eligibility & Partnership

Who can apply, and with whom

GGI is open to non-profit, legally registered organisations from one of the six Contracting Parties. Partnership across the region is the central design principle, not an optional extra.

Eligible organisations

The Lead Applicant must be a legal entity, non-profit, established in one of the six Contracting Parties, and fall into one of the following categories:

  • Civil society organisations (CSOs / NGOs), including grassroots groups
  • Local or regional public entities, or associations of them
  • Business associations — chambers of commerce, tourism, agriculture, etc.
  • Educational institutions — universities, faculties, schools, libraries, research centres, academies
  • Media associations — journalism and freedom of expression
  • Cultural & sports institutions — museums, galleries, theatres, libraries, associations

Partnership requirements

Partnership is mandatory. The structure tells the story of how a project is genuinely regional, and it is the first thing evaluators check.

  • Minimum of 3 different Contracting Parties represented
  • One Lead Applicant + at least two Project Partners, each from a different CP
  • Partnerships from more than 3 CPs are encouraged and may be prioritised in evaluation
  • The Lead Applicant signs the grant contract with WBF and is responsible for implementation and reporting
Applications per organisation
  • An organisation may submit multiple applications (as Lead or Partner)
  • But may receive only one grant as Lead and one as Partner, with the highest-scoring application taken in each case
Funding & Duration

How much, how long, what counts as a cost

GGI grants are designed for compact, well-focused regional actions: small enough to be delivered by grassroots teams, large enough to enable a real multi-country project.

Grant size & co-financing

Maximum grant per project€15,000
WBF grant covers up to80%of total eligible costs
Co-financing required≥ 20%of total eligible costs
Duration3–6 monthsincl. 30-day reporting
WBF grant: up to 80% Co-financing: minimum 20%
Worked example

A project requesting the maximum €15,000 grant needs at least €3,750 in co-financing, for a total budget of €18,750.

What counts as a cost

All costs must be necessary for the project, incurred during the implementation period, and properly justified in the budget.

  • Human resources — gross salaries, social charges. Capped at 30% of the WBF grant
  • Travel, accommodation and board — accommodation and board up to €100/night; personal vehicle at €0.30/km if justified
  • Other direct costs — venue rental, equipment, services, supplies for the action
  • Indirect costs — flat-rate, capped at 7% of total direct eligible costs
Not eligible
  • In-kind contributions; activities before contract signature
  • Equipment, land or buildings; bonuses; alcohol & tobacco
  • Sub-granting / financial support to third parties
  • VAT (unless non-recoverable under national law); fines; debts
How to Apply

The application process, step by step

Applications are submitted through the WBF Online Grant Management System (OGMS), the only accepted channel. Applications sent by email, fax or post are automatically rejected.

1
Find partners
Build a partnership across at least 3 Contracting Parties. The Lead Applicant and project partners must come from different Contracting Parties.
2
Register on OGMS
The Lead Applicant creates a profile on the WBF Online Grant Management System and completes the experience cards (or selects "No Experience" for grassroots organisations).
Register on OGMS
3
Submit the application
Complete Annex A (Application Form), Annex B (Budget) and Annex C (Declaration by the Lead Applicant, signed and stamped). All in English.
4
Supporting documents
If shortlisted, provide Partnership Agreement (WBF format), statute, registration certificate, bank proof, financial statements and financial-capacity form.

Inquiries should be sent to info@westernbalkansfund.org no later than 15 days before the deadline. Replies are summarised and published on the WBF website.

Evaluation & Selection

How proposals are scored

Applications first pass an administrative and eligibility check. Those that pass are scored against seven criteria for a total of 100 points by evaluators, then recommended for funding by the WBF Governing Bodies, ensuring balanced geographical distribution.

Minimum threshold: Applications scoring below 55 / 100 are not considered further. Final selection also respects balanced distribution of funds across the six Contracting Parties.
