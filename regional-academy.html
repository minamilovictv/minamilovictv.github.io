About the Academy

Why the Regional Academy, and why now

The Western Balkans remains one of the regions with the greatest need for a new culture of cooperation grounded in dialogue, trust-building, and intercultural understanding. The Regional Academy responds directly to that need.

Despite ongoing reforms and international support, the region continues to face structural, institutional, and socio-political challenges that limit democratic development, social cohesion, and lasting peace. Public discourse is still shaped by historical divisions, national narratives, and mutual mistrust, while citizens, and particularly young people, women, and minority communities, remain underrepresented in decision-making.

Emerging professionals often lack structured platforms to practice dialogue, exchange across differences, and engage in joint problem-solving. The Regional Academy was designed to fill that gap through joint learning, peer exchange, mentoring, and experiential engagement.

Although non-formal education programmes and leadership academies have increased significantly across the region, the WBF Regional Academy is distinguished by its integrated, practice-oriented, and regionally embedded design. Unlike traditional approaches that prioritise individual leadership development, the Academy places dialogue and cooperation at its core: it bridges critical reflection with experiential engagement so participants simultaneously learn, apply, and co-create solutions in real time.

Leadership, in this context, is understood not as a title or a position, but as a natural outcome of those who have genuinely learned to listen, cooperate, and through dialogue build trust across differences.

What makes the Academy different

An academy you experience, not just attend

  • 1Dialogue at the core: not as a topic to study, but as a daily practice that runs through every lecture, workshop, and evening session.
  • 2Maximum 30% lecture time. The rest is interactive workshops, dialogue sessions, site visits, and co-creation labs.
  • 3Tangible final products: group policy briefs, regional initiative concepts, and articles for the annual publication, not just certificates.
  • 4Pre and post mentorship. The programme starts before arrival and continues well after; it does not end with a closing ceremony.
  • 5Difficult conversations on the table. Memory, identity, and regional tensions are engaged with directly, in a safe and facilitated environment.

Methodology

Four learning tracks, one daily rhythm

Morning

Thematic Lectures

Expert-led morning sessions on the five thematic strands, structured as opening → presentation → break → interactive discussion → participatory closing.

Afternoon

Interactive Sessions

Afternoon workshops in flexible, mixed-CP groups. Highly participatory, non-formal education methods linked directly to the morning's themes.

  • Role-plays and simulations
  • Case study analysis & problem-solving
  • Co-creation lab: policy brief & initiative sprint
Evening

Dialogue Sessions

Evening sessions in informal settings such as terraces, gardens, and lounges that shift the atmosphere from classroom to community. Unstructured by design, facilitated lightly.

  • Fireside leadership conversations
  • Photography as dialogue
Field day

Site Visits

Heritage day combining classroom learning with experiential engagement, reflection, and dialogue across different dimensions of cultural memory and identity.

The Cohort

Who the Academy is built for?

The Regional Academy is designed for junior professionals and emerging leaders from across the WB6: people who are eager to grow beyond their national context, build lasting regional relationships, and develop practical dialogue and cooperation skills that are recognised across the region.

The cohort is deliberately mixed. Lawyers and activists work alongside artists and journalists. Civil servants exchange with entrepreneurs and researchers. Diversity is not an add-on; it is the precondition for the kind of dialogue the Academy is designed to enable.

Junior professionals Citizens of one of the WB6 Contracting Parties, early-to-mid career.
Mixed disciplines Activists, artists, journalists, lawyers, civil servants, researchers, entrepreneurs.
Regional outlook Eager to think beyond their national context and build lasting cross-border relationships.
Action-oriented Ready to commit to pre, during, and post-Academy work, including a group policy brief or initiative.
Heritage visits

The field-learning day that ties everything together

One full day outside the classroom, combining visits to sites that represent different historical periods and dimensions of identity, memory, and social transformation.

A day of heritage, memory, and reflection

Participants take part in a full-day heritage visit that combines guided site visits, structured reflection, and informal intercultural exchange. The day is designed as an extended learning exercise rather than a touristic activity: participants receive a background pack with historical context and guiding questions before departure, and facilitators lead reflection sessions throughout the day that connect what is seen on site with the Academy's themes of leadership, democracy, peacebuilding, and regional cooperation. The day concludes with a shared meal and a group reflection on what the experience has revealed about memory, identity, and cooperation across the region.

Heritage Values Framework

At each site, participants assess and discuss the different values represented, recognising that different communities assign different meanings to heritage, and that this diversity of perspectives is itself an important learning outcome.
