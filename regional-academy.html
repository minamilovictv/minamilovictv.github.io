About the Academy

Why the Regional Academy, and why now

The Western Balkans remains one of the regions with the greatest need for a new culture of cooperation grounded in dialogue, trust-building, and intercultural understanding. The Regional Academy responds directly to that need.

Despite ongoing reforms and international support, the region continues to face structural, institutional, and socio-political challenges that limit democratic development, social cohesion, and lasting peace. Public discourse is still shaped by historical divisions, national narratives, and mutual mistrust, while citizens, and particularly young people, women, and minority communities, remain underrepresented in decision-making.

Emerging professionals often lack structured platforms to practice dialogue, exchange across differences, and engage in joint problem-solving. The Regional Academy was designed to fill that gap through joint learning, peer exchange, mentoring, and experiential engagement.

Although non-formal education programmes and leadership academies have increased significantly across the region, the WBF Regional Academy is distinguished by its integrated, practice-oriented, and regionally embedded design. Unlike traditional approaches that prioritise individual leadership development, the Academy places dialogue and cooperation at its core: it bridges critical reflection with experiential engagement so participants simultaneously learn, apply, and co-create solutions in real time.

Leadership, in this context, is understood not as a title or a position, but as a natural outcome of those who have genuinely learned to listen, cooperate, and through dialogue build trust across differences.

What makes the Academy different

An academy you experience, not just attend

  • 1Dialogue at the core: not as a topic to study, but as a daily practice that runs through every lecture, workshop, and evening session.
  • 2Maximum 30% lecture time. The rest is interactive workshops, dialogue sessions, site visits, and co-creation labs.
  • 3Tangible final products: group policy briefs, regional initiative concepts, and articles for the annual publication, not just certificates.
  • 4Pre and post mentorship. The programme starts before arrival and continues well after; it does not end with a closing ceremony.
  • 5Difficult conversations on the table. Memory, identity, and regional tensions are engaged with directly, in a safe and facilitated environment.
Thematic Pillars

Five threads that run through the week

The full programme is structured around five interconnected thematic pillars. Every lecture, session, and site visit is anchored in at least one of them.

PILLAR 01
Democratic Citizenship & Governance
Core principles and skills for effective leadership, including accountability, transparency, and citizen participation, and how to engage with institutions and decision-making.
PILLAR 02
Gender Equality & Inclusive Leadership
Structural and cultural barriers to equal participation, and the tools to advance gender-responsive leadership and women's inclusion in peacebuilding.
PILLAR 03
Freedom of Expression & Democratic Participation
The role of free expression in democratic societies, challenges to media freedom and civic space, and how to advocate for and protect these rights.
PILLAR 04
Regional Cooperation & Peacebuilding
Interdependencies across WB6 Contracting Parties and joint regional solutions that strengthen trust, social cohesion, reconciliation, and joint development.
PILLAR 05
Intercultural Understanding & Cooperation Culture
Both theoretical frameworks and practical case studies: analysing regional dynamics while developing inclusive, actionable solutions together.

Explores the historical evolution and contemporary dynamics of the Western Balkans, with a particular focus on the legacy of the 1990s conflicts and the ongoing processes of peacebuilding and regional cooperation. Provides participants with a structured analytical framework to understand how historical experiences, collective memory, and identity narratives continue to shape political relations, social cohesion, and trust across the region.

Objectives

  • Develop a critical understanding of conflicts, peace processes, and post-conflict transformation in the Western Balkans.
  • Analyse the state of social inclusion and cohesion, and assess the achievements and limits of frameworks like RCC, RYCO, WBF, and CEFTA.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explore the role of collective memory and historical narratives in shaping community relationships.
  • Analyse challenges and opportunities in post-conflict reconciliation.
  • Reflect on the role of inclusive remembrance, education, culture, and civil society in building sustainable peace.

Examines dialogue as both a theoretical framework and a practical tool for transforming relationships in divided societies. Distinguishes genuine, transformative dialogue from ordinary conversation, focusing on its role in post-conflict and multicultural contexts where competing narratives, identity differences, and historical sensitivities require structured and facilitated communication processes.

Objectives

  • Understand the core principles and dimensions of dialogue: what enables meaningful exchange beyond superficial conversation.
  • Analyse how dialogue can be used as a tool for trust-building, conflict transformation, and cooperation in divided or post-conflict societies.

Key Readings

  • David Bohm, On Dialogue
  • William Isaacs, Dialogue: The Art of Thinking Together
  • John Paul Lederach, Building Peace
  • Harold Saunders, A Public Peace Process

Examines the architecture of governance and cooperation in the Western Balkans, focusing on the interplay between formal institutions, regional organizations, and civil society actors. Encourages participants to understand governance not as a top-down institutional system, but as a multi-layered ecosystem where state actors, regional bodies, and civil society jointly shape outcomes.

Objectives

  • Understand the structure and functioning of governance systems and regional cooperation mechanisms in the Western Balkans.
  • Analyse the role of institutions and civil society in promoting democratic governance, accountability, and regional cooperation.

Learning Outcomes

  • Identify key regional governance frameworks and their significance for regional development.
  • Evaluate challenges and opportunities for cooperation among institutions, civil society, and regional actors.
  • Recognise the importance of inclusive governance and multi-stakeholder cooperation.

Explores how media, communication, and public narratives shape perceptions of cooperation, conflict, and identity in the Western Balkans. Examines how information ecosystems influence public opinion, reinforce or challenge stereotypes, and contribute to either polarisation or social cohesion. Particular focus on media freedom and the role of journalists, civil society actors, and communicators in the public dimension of peacebuilding.

Objectives

  • Understand how media, communication, and narratives shape perceptions of cooperation, identity, and conflict.
  • Analyse the role of traditional and digital media in framing public discourse, including misinformation and polarisation.

Learning Outcomes

  • Critically assess the impact of disinformation, stereotypes, and biased narratives on social cohesion.
  • Identify strategies for promoting balanced, inclusive narratives.
  • Develop a critical understanding of the responsibilities of media professionals and citizens in public discourse.

Examines leadership not as positional authority, but as a set of values, behaviours, and practices grounded in accountability, empathy, integrity, and the ability to foster trust in diverse and polarised societies. Connects human rights as both a normative framework and a practical tool with the concept of democratic resilience and the role of emerging leaders in safeguarding democratic values.

Objectives

  • Understand leadership as a value-based and relational practice connected to human rights and democratic governance.
  • Analyse democratic resilience and the role of emerging leaders in strengthening institutions, civic space, and social cohesion.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the relationship between leadership, human rights, and societal resilience.
  • Assess challenges to democracy, including polarisation, discrimination, and democratic backsliding.
  • Reflect on one's own potential as a leader and active citizen advancing democratic values.

Explores the role of women, youth, and marginalised groups in shaping more inclusive, equitable, and sustainable models of regional cooperation. Examines structural and cultural barriers to participation in public, political, and economic life while highlighting the transformative contributions of women's leadership and inclusive movements. Forward-looking dimension on youth engagement, digital transformation, and civic innovation.

Objectives

  • Understand inclusion, gender equality, and intersectionality as a foundation for sustainable cooperation and democratic governance.
  • Analyse the barriers and opportunities for women's leadership in shaping future regional cooperation.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the importance of gender equality in advancing cooperation and sustainable development.
  • Identify barriers limiting the participation of women and underrepresented groups.
  • Evaluate good practices that promote gender-responsive approaches to development.
Methodology

Four learning tracks, one daily rhythm

Thematic Lectures
Expert-led morning sessions on the six thematic modules, structured as opening → presentation → break → interactive discussion → participatory closing.
Visual materials, case studies, video testimonies Critical examination of inherited narratives Co-creation between expert and participants
Interactive Sessions
Afternoon workshops in flexible, mixed-CP groups. Highly participatory, non-formal education methods linked directly to the morning's themes.
Role-plays and simulations Case study analysis & problem-solving Co-creation lab: policy brief & initiative sprint
Dialogue Sessions
Evening sessions in informal settings such as terraces, gardens, and lounges that shift the atmosphere from classroom to community. Unstructured by design, facilitated lightly.
Fireside leadership conversations Photography as dialogue
Site Visits
Heritage day combining classroom learning with experiential engagement, reflection, and dialogue across different dimensions of cultural memory and identity.
Kruja: Skanderbeg Museum & Old Bazaar Bunk'Art 1: Cold War memory institution Reflection dinner on Dajti Mountain

The Cohort

Who the Academy is built for?

The Regional Academy is designed for junior professionals and emerging leaders from across the WB6: people who are eager to grow beyond their national context, build lasting regional relationships, and develop practical dialogue and cooperation skills that are recognised across the region.

The cohort is deliberately mixed. Lawyers and activists work alongside artists and journalists. Civil servants exchange with entrepreneurs and researchers. Diversity is not an add-on; it is the precondition for the kind of dialogue the Academy is designed to enable.

Junior professionals Citizens of one of the WB6 Contracting Parties, early-to-mid career.
Mixed disciplines Activists, artists, journalists, lawyers, civil servants, researchers, entrepreneurs.
Regional outlook Eager to think beyond their national context and build lasting cross-border relationships.
Action-oriented Ready to commit to pre, during, and post-Academy work, including a group policy brief or initiative.

Heritage Day · 19 September

The field-learning day that ties everything together

One full day outside the classroom, combining visits to sites that represent different historical periods and dimensions of identity, memory, and social transformation.

SAT 19 SEP · Day 7

A day of heritage, memory, and reflection

Participants take part in a full-day heritage visit that combines guided site visits, structured reflection, and informal intercultural exchange. The day is designed as an extended learning exercise rather than a touristic activity: participants receive a background pack with historical context and guiding questions before departure, and facilitators lead reflection sessions throughout the day that connect what is seen on site with the Academy's themes of leadership, democracy, peacebuilding, and regional cooperation. The day concludes with a shared meal and a group reflection on what the experience has revealed about memory, identity, and cooperation across the region.

Heritage Values Framework

At each site, participants assess and discuss the different values represented, recognising that different communities assign different meanings to heritage, and that this diversity of perspectives is itself an important learning outcome.

Historical Archaeological Architectural Aesthetic Sense of Place Spiritual Community Recreational Ecological Political Identity Associative Economic Educational
