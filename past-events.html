WBF Advocacy & Networking Events

Building on its experience on regional cooperation and dialogue events, the Western Balkans Fund has become a recognized, visible and active promotor of civil society actors across the Western Balkans. Together with its longstanding partners, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan and the Embassy of Japan in Tirana, the Western Balkans Fund hosts events addressing critical issues related to regional cooperation, dialogue, and reconciliation. These themed and WBF-facilitated events involve relevant stakeholders not only from the region and European Union, but also from all over the world.

Western Balkans Diaspora

#LeadBalkans Conference

February 2026 Tirana, Albania

Held in Tirana in February 2026, the #LeadBalkans Conference was a flagship regional event dedicated to strengthening cooperation between the Western Balkans and its diaspora communities.

3
days
6
panels
100
diaspora and community leaders participated

The conference was built around a clear premise: while regional cooperation in the Western Balkans is becoming stronger and more structured, diaspora communities are still frequently left outside these processes.

Therefore, their knowledge, professional experience, international connections and capacity to contribute to the region’s development remain insufficiently integrated into regional initiatives and policymaking.

To address this gap, the Western Balkans Fund brought together more than 100 diaspora representatives from across the Western Balkans. Participants travelled to Tirana from over 20 countries, stretching from the United States to Japan, reflecting the extraordinary geographical reach and diversity of the region’s diaspora.

Diaspora is the best ambassador of our culture and a crucial partner in supporting development and strengthening vital connections between our region and the wider world.

Ervin IbrahimovićDeputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs of Montenegro

Many previous discussions on diaspora engagement have focused primarily on the concepts of “brain drain” and “brain gain”: how to prevent talented people from leaving the region, how to encourage them to return, or how to attract their knowledge and investments back to their countries of origin.

The #LeadBalkans Conference, however, was designed around a broader and more practical vision. It explored how borders should not become barriers preventing people from participating fully in the economic, political and social life of a country or region. Diaspora communities can contribute not only to the societies from which they originate, but to the Western Balkans as a whole.

This regional dimension is particularly important in the context of European integration. The experience of diaspora members who live, work and participate in democratic societies around the world can provide valuable knowledge, perspectives and networks for a region undergoing profound political, economic and social transformation.

We have entered a moment in which diaspora has been placed at the center of strategic thinking about our European future.

Miriam ShytiDeputy Minister of Europe and Foreign Affairs, Albania

Throughout the conference sessions, participants shared their experiences and offered concrete feedback on how institutions can build more inclusive and sustainable mechanisms for diaspora engagement. Discussions addressed the participation of diaspora professionals in public and civic life, regional networking, investment, knowledge exchange, policymaking and cooperation across borders.

Outcome

The conference resulted in a set of recommendations addressed to the WBF Contracting Parties, outlining practical steps for improving diaspora engagement at both national and regional levels.

More importantly, #LeadBalkans created a lasting network of professionals, community leaders and organisations committed to contributing to the future of the Western Balkans. The Western Balkans Fund will continue to support this network, facilitate cooperation among its members and promote the meaningful inclusion of diaspora communities in regional initiatives.

The conference demonstrated that the diaspora should not be viewed only through the lens of departure or return. It is already an active part of the region’s present and can become an even stronger partner in shaping its European future.

Local Government & Regional Cooperation

SYNERGY Conference

5–7 March 2025 Hotel Maritim Plaza, Tirana, Albania

Held in Tirana from 5 to 7 March 2025, the SYNERGY Conference was a flagship regional gathering dedicated to strengthening the role of local governments in regional cooperation, sustainable development and the European integration of the Western Balkans.

3
days
6
panels
9
participating ministers
60
mayors and representatives of local municipalities
150+

The conference was built around a clear idea: many of the region’s most important challenges are experienced locally, but their causes and solutions often extend beyond municipal and national borders. Economic development, environmental protection, demographic decline, digital transformation and social inclusion cannot be addressed effectively by municipalities working in isolation.

For this reason, the Western Balkans Fund brought together nine ministers, more than 60 mayors and municipal representatives, alongside international organisations, civil society actors and development partners from the Western Balkans and the Visegrad region.

Municipalities are the foundation of successful European Integration.

Igli HasaniMinister of Europe and Foreign Affairs of Albania

The SYNERGY Conference moved beyond the traditional understanding of municipalities as administrative bodies. Instead, it presented them as active engines of economic development, innovation, sustainability and cross-border cooperation.

Although national governments lead the formal process of European integration, its practical impact is felt most directly at the local level. Municipalities are where policies become services, infrastructure and visible improvements in people’s everyday lives. They are also the institutions closest to citizens and are therefore essential for rebuilding trust, encouraging participation and turning regional cooperation into concrete results.

We must rethink the role of municipalities. They should not be limited to basic infrastructure and public services. They must be the real engines of development.

Arbjan MaznikuMinister of State for Local Government in Albania

Throughout the conference sessions, participants exchanged experiences and good practices related to local economic development, environmental protection, innovation, sustainable urban planning and the implementation of European integration reforms. The discussions also examined the challenges faced by municipalities, including limited institutional capacities, demographic pressures, insufficient access to financing and the need for stronger cooperation with civil society and the private sector.

A central message emerged from these exchanges: municipalities across the Western Balkans often face similar problems, yet they do not need to search for solutions alone. Regional cooperation allows local authorities to learn from one another, develop joint initiatives, exchange expertise and build partnerships capable of producing measurable benefits for their communities.

The participation of representatives from the Visegrad countries added an important European dimension to the conference. Their experience demonstrated how cooperation among local authorities can contribute to institutional development, improve public services and support broader processes of European integration.

Initiatives like the Visegrad and the Western Balkans Fund demonstrate the real power of the cooperation.

Jan LipavskýMinister of Foreign Affairs of the Czech Republic
Outcome

The conference resulted in stronger connections among municipalities, national authorities, regional organisations and development partners. It also generated practical recommendations for improving local governance, expanding municipal cooperation and increasing the participation of local authorities in regional and European initiatives.

More importantly, SYNERGY established a platform for lasting partnerships across borders. The Western Balkans Fund will continue to support these connections and promote initiatives that place municipalities at the centre of regional cooperation.

The conference demonstrated that European integration cannot be achieved only through negotiations between capitals. It must also take place in cities, towns and local communities, where cooperation becomes tangible and where citizens can see its real value in their daily lives.

Freedom of Expression & Disinformation

VerityCon23: Navigating the Disinformation Era

14–16 November 2023 Tirana, Albania

Held in Tirana from 14 to 16 November 2023, VerityCon23: Navigating the Disinformation Era was a flagship regional conference dedicated to freedom of expression and the growing challenge of disinformation in the Western Balkans.

3
days
6
panels
60+
experts, journalists, policymakers and civil society representatives

Organised by the Western Balkans Fund with the support of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan and the International Visegrad Fund, the conference brought together more than 60 experts, journalists, policymakers and civil society representatives from the Western Balkans, the Visegrad countries, Japan, the United States and European Union member states.

The conference was built around a clear idea: access to information has never been wider, but distinguishing reliable information from deliberate manipulation has rarely been more difficult. Digital platforms have enabled ideas and news to travel across borders within seconds, but they have also created an environment in which false narratives, propaganda and manipulated content can spread faster than facts.

For the Western Balkans, this challenge carries particular significance. In societies still affected by political polarisation, unresolved divisions and low levels of institutional trust, disinformation can deepen existing tensions, weaken democratic debate and undermine regional cooperation. It can also distort public understanding of reforms and weaken support for the European integration process.

Overall goal

To provide a platform for open discussion among a diverse group of stakeholders, with an emphasis on fostering the right to freely express ideas in relation to combating false information, misleading content, and deliberate falsehoods, including impacts from external sources.

Specific objective

To further facilitate the analysis of the situation regarding the topic, identifying gaps, highlighting the available and required capabilities to address, prevent, contain and reduce the impact of fake news, misinformation and disinformation around the region.

VerityCon23 therefore moved beyond the narrow understanding of disinformation as simply a media problem. It approached it as a broader democratic and societal challenge requiring coordinated action from governments, journalists, civil society organisations, educational institutions, digital platforms and citizens themselves.

Across three days, participants examined some of the most difficult questions shaping the contemporary information environment. They discussed how to recognise disinformation, propaganda, conspiracy theories, pseudoscience, clickbait, automated accounts and manipulated content. The sessions also explored how images, videos and information circulating through websites and social media can be verified, and how individuals and organisations can respond without unintentionally amplifying false narratives.

Through six panels and interactive buzz sessions, the conference combined strategic discussion with practical knowledge. Participants explored digital safety, media literacy, fact-checking, the protection of journalists, the responsibility of public institutions and the complex balance between regulation and freedom of expression.

Key message

A central message emerged from the discussions: disinformation cannot be addressed solely by correcting individual falsehoods after they appear. A sustainable response must also confront the conditions in which disinformation thrives, including political polarisation, social divisions, weak independent media, limited transparency and declining public trust.

Gender Equality & Women’s Leadership

UNSTOPPABLE International Conference

20–23 February 2023 Tirana, Albania

Held in Tirana from 20 to 23 February 2023, the UNSTOPPABLE International Conference was a flagship regional gathering dedicated to gender equality, women’s empowerment and equal access to leadership.

4
days
12
sessions
150+
participants
60+
speakers

Organised by the Western Balkans Fund with the financial support of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, the International Visegrad Fund and Forum 2000, the four-day conference brought together more than 150 participants and over 60 speakers from more than 20 countries. They represented governments, civil society, international organisations, academia, the media and the business community across the Western Balkans, the Visegrad region, Japan, Switzerland and other European countries.

The conference was built around a clear idea: although women’s participation in public, political and economic life has increased, equal access to leadership remains far from achieved.

Women continue to face structural barriers, including unequal access to professional networks, financial resources, mentoring and decision-making positions, as well as discrimination, sexist language and institutional cultures that preserve established models of leadership.

Purpose

UNSTOPPABLE was therefore conceived as a practical space for examining how governments, businesses and civil society organisations can create more equal conditions for women to lead and influence development processes.

Across 12 sessions, participants explored women’s leadership, democratic participation, financial literacy, peace and security, sexist hate speech and the challenges faced by emerging young leaders.

Through panel discussions, mentoring sessions and smaller working circles, participants exchanged experiences and received practical guidance on communication, negotiation, professional development and building sustainable networks. The conference emphasised that empowerment cannot be reduced to inspiration alone, but requires skills, resources, institutional reform and long-term support.

Outcome

More importantly, UNSTOPPABLE strengthened a regional and international network of women and men committed to advancing gender equality. It connected established leaders with younger professionals, civil society with public institutions and regional experience with international expertise.

Freedom of Expression & Hate Speech

FIRE: Promoting a Culture of Tolerance and Mutual Respect

1–3 March 2022 Hotel Tirana International, Tirana, Albania

Held in Tirana from 1 to 3 March 2022, FIRE was an inter-regional conference dedicated to freedom of expression and to the response to hate speech and defamation across the Western Balkans and the Visegrad region.

3
days
7
panel discussions
43
panellists from both regions
84
participants on site
3302
followed the sessions online

Organised by the Western Balkans Fund with the support of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan and the International Visegrad Fund, the conference brought together media professionals, government representatives, think tanks, civil society organisations, self-regulatory bodies and judicial institutions from the Western Balkans, the Visegrad countries and European Union member states.

The conference was built around a clear idea: hate speech is widely condemned, but it is still loosely defined. Offensive language targeting ethnic, religious and sexual minorities, migrants and other groups is a widespread phenomenon in the region, yet there is little consistency in how it is described, measured or addressed. Without a shared frame of reference, responses to it remain short sighted and fragmented.

For the Western Balkans, this carries particular weight. In societies still affected by unresolved divisions, hate speech and defamation weaken social cohesion, narrow public debate and hold back regional cooperation. At the same time, any response has to protect freedom of expression itself, which necessarily covers speech that offends, shocks or disturbs.

Overall goal

To promote freedom of expression as a fundamental human right and to introduce legal mechanisms addressing hate speech and defamation in the Western Balkans and the Visegrad regions.

Specific objective

To create an ecosystem that encourages open dialogue among stakeholders addressing all forms of hate speech and defamation.

The programme was organised around three working spaces, each designed for a different kind of conversation. The Joint Space opened and closed the conference and was open to every participant on site and online. The Dynamic Space was built as a plenary that gave equal weight to those in the room and those joining remotely, so that travel and health restrictions did not decide who could take part. The Fixed Space was a roundtable for a registered specialist audience, held under the Chatham House Rule, where panellists sat among participants and worked directly on recommendations.

A spectrum, not a single category

A recurring conclusion across the panels was that hate speech cannot be treated as one uniform offence. Participants distinguished between:

  • expression that is severe enough to carry criminal liability;
  • expression that falls below that threshold but is subject to civil or administrative liability;
  • expression that carries no legal liability but still causes prejudice and harm, and has to be addressed through non-legal means such as education, media literacy, self-regulation and public debate.

Across the seven panels, participants examined hate speech in political communication, online hate speech and cyber bullying among young people, sexist hate speech against women, the risks of defamation and the misuse of litigation against journalists, the work of media regulators and self-regulatory bodies, and the effect of hate speech on regional cooperation.

Key message

Responses to hate speech have to be proportionate to the harm they address. Legal instruments are necessary but insufficient on their own, and measures that are too broad risk becoming a tool for silencing opposition. As speakers put it during the opening session, the task is to minimise compulsion and maximise persuasion.

Outcome

The conference produced a set of recommendations and conclusions addressed to governments, media regulators, self-regulatory bodies, civil society organisations, the donor community and academia, and presented in a final report. The recommendations were framed to support both the protection of freedom of expression and the reduction of hate speech and defamation in public discourse.

FIRE also widened the circle of people working on this agenda. Around 87 per cent of participants were attending a Western Balkans Fund event for the first time, and 93 per cent considered the topics highly important for the region. Beyond the room, the visibility campaign around the conference reached more than 55,000 people on social media and drew a further 2,700 visitors to the Fund’s website.

More importantly, the conference established the Western Balkans Fund as a convener on freedom of expression and connected media organisations, institutions and associations that had not previously worked together across borders. That network carried directly into the Fund’s later work on the same agenda, including UNSTOPPABLE in 2023 and VerityCon23 later that year.

Civil Society & Regional Cooperation

1st Western Balkans Civil Society Summit

27–29 February 2020 Tirana, Albania

Held in Tirana over three working days, the first Western Balkans Civil Society Summit brought together more than 100 civil society representatives from across the region to discuss the future of the sector and its role in strengthening regional cooperation.

Organised by the Western Balkans Fund with the support of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, the Summit was the first gathering of its kind convened by the Fund and an important milestone in the regional cooperation process. Preparations began months in advance, and participants were selected through a rigorous process that brought experienced practitioners from every part of the region into the room.

The Summit was built around a clear idea: civil society organisations across the Western Balkans work on the same problems, often in isolation from one another. Bringing them together with the institutions that shape their operating environment allows challenges and achievements to be examined side by side, and turns individual experience into shared regional knowledge.

Across the three days, panels brought together senior government representatives, international and regional organisations and civil society leaders to examine the obstacles the sector faces and the results it has achieved so far.

Opening and panels

The Summit was greeted by Seiichiro Taguchi, Director of the Central and South-Eastern Europe Division at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan. Opening remarks were also delivered by Nenad Koprivica, Director General at the Ministry of Sports and Youth of Montenegro, Zoran Popov, Secretary of State at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of North Macedonia, Mimi Kodheli, Chair of the Foreign Affairs Committee of the Assembly of the Republic of Albania, and Andor F. Dávid, Executive Director of the International Visegrad Fund.

Regional and international organisations were represented in the panel discussions by Fatos Mustafa, Deputy Secretary General of RYCO, Juliana Hoxha, Executive Director of Partners Albania, Bojana Dujković, Board Member of EUROCLIO in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Uranik Begu, Executive Director of the Innovation Centre Kosovo, and Irina Ljubić, Executive Director of the Jelena Šantić Foundation. They were joined by Jelica Minić, Vice President of the European Movement in Serbia, Bardhyl Jashari, Executive Director of the Metamorphosis Foundation, Mirela Rajković, Executive Director of SEEYN, and Igor Milošević, Programme Manager at ADP ZID in Montenegro.

Working groups

The second day was given over entirely to the participants. Four working groups met to develop recommendations for the governments of the Contracting Parties, organised around priority areas for the Fund: education and scientific exchanges, cultural cooperation, sustainable development, and a dedicated group for think tanks. Each group produced its own set of recommendations, which were presented directly to government representatives and published afterwards.

Outcome

The recommendations were received by Oltion Rrumbullaku, Deputy Minister of Education, Sports and Youth of Albania, Almir Šahović, Assistant Minister and Ambassador at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Pavle Janković, Head of the Department for Regional Initiatives at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Serbia, Sokol Dedja, Deputy Minister for Europe and Foreign Affairs of Albania, and Makoto Ito, Ambassador of Japan to Albania. They committed to a more supportive approach from their governments and to working with the Western Balkans Fund to strengthen regional cooperation.

The Summit established a format the Fund has returned to since. It demonstrated that civil society organisations in the Western Balkans are not only implementers of projects, but partners in shaping the regional agenda, and that a structured exchange between them and their governments produces commitments that outlast the event itself.
