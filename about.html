The Fund

Who we are

The Western Balkans Fund (WBF) is an intergovernmental organisation based in Tirana, Albania, established by the governments of Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo*, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia. Conceived as a fully regionally owned and all-inclusive initiative, the Fund stands as a tangible expression of a renewed spirit of cooperation in the Western Balkans, a mechanism created by the region, for the region, to bring its people, institutions and communities closer together.

WBF is the first home-grown regional mechanism that boost cooperation through grants for civil society organisations and other stakeholders in the Western Balkans Six. Its founding reflects a shared conviction that lasting reconciliation and a common European future are built not only between governments, but between citizens, civil-society organisations and communities working side by side on concrete, cross-border projects.

*This designation is without prejudice to positions on status and is in line with UNSCR 1244 and the ICJ Opinion on the Kosovo declaration of independence.

Our Mission

The Western Balkans Fund is dedicated to promoting reconciliation, strengthening regional cooperation, and fostering closer ties among the Contracting Parties of the Western Balkans. Through its initiatives, the Fund contributes to building trust, dialogue, and sustainable partnerships across the region.

Values

The Western Balkans Fund operates in full alignment with the vision of its founding members, supporting the principles of reconciliation, regional solidarity, and the shared European future of all Contracting Parties. Our work is guided by cooperation, inclusiveness, transparency, and mutual respect.

History

WBF establishment

The Agreement for the creation of the Western Balkans Fund was signed by the Ministers of Foreign Affairs of the Western Balkans Six in November 2015.

Following the completion of ratification procedures in all six national parliaments, the Fund became operational on 1 October 2017.

The Fund is financed by its six Contracting Parties in equal quotas, ensuring balanced ownership and shared responsibility across the region. Its founding Executive Director, elected by the WBF Conference of Ministers of Foreign Affairs, is Mr Gjergj Murra, a career diplomat from Albania, who continues to lead the organisation. The Secretariat, seated in Tirana, brings together experts from across the Western Balkans, reflecting the regional character of the institution at every level.

Governance is exercised through the Conference of Ministers of Foreign Affairs as the Fund's highest body, supported by the Council of Senior Officials and the Secretariat. The presidency of the Fund rotates annually among the Contracting Parties; in 2026 it is held by Montenegro.

History

Key Milestones

From the founding agreement in 2015 to a new generation of programmes — a decade of building regional cooperation across the Western Balkans.

  1. November 2015

    Agreement on WBF establishment signed

    The Foreign Ministers of the Western Balkans Six sign the founding agreement, creating the region’s first home-grown Fund, a regionally owned mechanism aiming to boost cooperation on a wide scale.

  1. March 2016

    Meeting of the Foreign Ministers of the Western Balkans

    The WB6 Ministers convene to advance ratification and shape the new Fund’s mandate and structure.

  1. October 2017

    WBF becomes operational

    After ratification by all six Parliaments, the Fund opens its Secretariat in Tirana and begins work.

  2. November 2017

    First Call for Proposals launched

    The Fund opens its inaugural annual grant scheme for regional civil-society projects. Over 300 applications received.

  1. April 2018

    Host Country Agreement signed

    WBF and Albania formalise the legal seat of the Secretariat in Tirana.

  2. November 2018

    Second Call for Proposals

    The annual grant scheme returns, expanding the Fund’s portfolio of regional projects.

  1. April 2019

    Third WBF Conference of Ministers of Foreign Affairs

    The Fund’s highest governing body meets to review progress and set strategic direction.

  2. July 2019

    WBF at the Berlin Process Summit in Poznań

    The Fund takes part in the Western Balkans Summit, anchoring its role in the regional cooperation agenda.

  3. November 2019

    Third Call for Proposals

    The third annual grant cycle opens for regional cooperation projects.

  1. February 2020

    Western Balkans Civil Society Summit

    WBF convenes civil-society actors from across the region to strengthen dialogue and partnership. This is the first Japanese-funded advocacy event and opened the door to many other advocacy events in the following period.

  2. December 2020

    Strategic Development Plan 2020–2024 approved

    The Fund adopts its first multi-year strategy, setting priorities for growth and impact.

  1. June 2021

    Switzerland becomes a WBF partner

    The Swiss Government begins supporting the Fund’s institutional strengthening and portfolio diversification.

  2. July 2021

    European Union becomes a WBF partner

    The EU and WBF launch a Joint Action (IPA), co-funding regional grants for civil-society and grassroots organisations.

  3. November 2021

    Fourth Call for Proposals launched

    The first EU-co-funded annual grant cycle opens.

  1. March 2022

    FIRE Conference against hate speech

    Co-funded by Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the International Visegrad Fund — the first project jointly backed by Japan and the IVF — the conference gathered over 70 experts in Tirana to confront hate speech and defamation.

  2. May 2022

    Fifth Call for Proposals

    The annual grant scheme opens for a new cohort of regional projects.

  1. February 2023

    Unstoppable International Conference

    Held on 20–23 February in Tirana with Japanese and Visegrad Fund support, the Conference on Women and Leadership brought together more than 120 participants and over 40 speakers, advancing a shared regional agenda for gender equality and women in decision-making.

  2. March 2023

    WBF Move Grants, First Call for Proposals

    The Fund launches its Swiss-supported individual-mobility scheme; 424 applicants begin the process and 23 regional projects are awarded.

  3. June 2023

    WBF Move Grants, Second Call for Proposals

    Open to individual applicants for the first time, the second call attracts 113 applications and awards 34 grants.

  4. October 2023

    WBF Move Grants, Third Call for Proposals

    The third round draws 202 applications, bringing the scheme’s total to 85 grants across its first three calls.

  5. November 2023

    VerityCon Conference

    Held on 14–16 November in Tirana with Japanese and Visegrad Fund support, “VerityCon2023: Navigating the Disinformation Era” was the region’s largest conference on freedom of expression, convening over 60 experts to counter disinformation.

  6. November 2023

    Sixth Call for Proposals

    The sixth annual grant cycle opens for regional cooperation projects.

  1. March 2024

    WBF Move Grants, Fourth Call for Proposals

    A new round of individual mobility grants opens across the WB6, drawing around 100 complete applications.

  2. May 2024

    AICS becomes a partner

    The Italian Agency for Development Cooperation (AICS) signs its first cooperation agreement with WBF, focused on cultural and creative industries.

  3. May 2024

    Empowering Regional Cooperation (ERC) Grants launched

    The Fund’s largest grant scheme yet opens, doubling the ceiling to €30,000; across its first two calls the scheme supports 24 regional projects.

  4. June 2024

    WB6-V4 Fellowship launched

    Jointly with the International Visegrad Fund, WBF launches a two-way academic mobility scheme; the first 41 fellows are announced.

  5. September 2024

    WBF Move Grants, Fifth Call for Proposals

    A further round of individual mobility grants opens, bringing the scheme’s cumulative total to 141 supported individuals.

  6. October 2024

    Regional Networking and Dialogue Session format launched

    A new dialogue and networking initiative under the WBF–EU Joint Action kicks off in four different capital cities.

  1. January 2025

    Seventh Call for Proposals

    The seventh annual grant cycle opens, co-funded by the EU (IPA III); it attracts 417 applications, with 30 projects selected — five from each Contracting Party.

  2. May 2025

    WB6-V4 Fellowship, second edition

    The joint WBF–IVF fellowship returns for a second round, bringing the total to 85 fellows across the first two editions.

  3. October 2025

    WBF Move Grants, Sixth Call for Proposals

    The sixth round of individual mobility grants opens, lifting the scheme’s cumulative total to 171 supported individuals.

  4. October 2025

    Berlin Process Summit in London

    WBF takes part in the UK-hosted Western Balkans summit (22 October 2025), where it commits to advancing gender equality across the region — a pledge that sets the stage for the launch of the Gender Equality Fund.

  1. January 2026

    Second Strategic Development Plan (2026–2030) adopted

    Building on the achievements of its first decade, WBF adopted its second Strategic Development Plan (2026–2030), setting the Fund’s vision for the next phase of institutional growth, programme innovation, strategic partnerships, and regional impact.

  2. February 2026

    Germany becomes a partner

    At the Montenegro presidency handover in Podgorica, WBF signs its first cooperation agreement with the Federal Republic of Germany, broadening the Fund’s circle of international partners.

  3. February 2026

    Switzerland partnership renewed

    At the same ceremony, the partnership with the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation is renewed for the period 2026–2029.

  4. May 2026

    Gender Equality Fund (GeFund) launched

    Delivering the commitment made in London, WBF opens its first dedicated gender-equality scheme, co-funded by the EU and Switzerland, with the first regional projects awarded in July 2026 and the historically largest ceiling for grants, up to €40,000.

  5. September 2026

    First We-Be Regional Academy

    Together with the Open Society Foundations – Western Balkans (OSF-WB), WBF launches a residential academy for emerging leaders on dialogue and a culture of cooperation.

WBF and the Berlin Process

WBF and the Berlin Process

The Western Balkans Fund has been directly linked with the regional cooperation, reconciliation, and people-to-people connectivity agenda of the Berlin Process.

This connection goes back to the early years of the Berlin Process. Three important milestones have marked this road.

  • 2025London
  • 2020Sofia
  • 2019Poznań
  1. 01
    2025

    London — From recognition to concrete responsibility

    The most recent milestone came in 2025 and represented perhaps the strongest recognition of WBF within the Berlin Process to date.

    On 22 October 2025, the Western Balkans Fund participated for the first time in a Berlin Process Leaders’ Summit, hosted by the United Kingdom in London.

    In Decision No. 46 of the Chair’s Conclusions, Berlin Process participants welcomed:

    “the decision by the WB6 that 15% of funds through the Western Balkans Fund will now be earmarked for initiatives on gender equality.”

    Chair’s Conclusions, Decision No. 46 · London Summit, 22 October 2025

    The decision formed part of a wider commitment to integrate gender equality throughout the Berlin Process agenda, recognising it as an enabler and accelerator of regional priorities and linking women’s empowerment with more secure, stable, and prosperous societies.

    Building upon the London Conclusions, WBF established the Western Balkans Gender Equality Fund, a dedicated regional mechanism supporting civil society cooperation, advocacy, and initiatives advancing women’s empowerment and gender equality. The first Open Call was launched on 5 May 2026, translating the political commitment adopted within the Berlin Process into direct support for organisations and communities across the Western Balkans.

    Berlin Process Leaders’ Summit — London, 22 October 2025.

  2. 02
    2020

    Sofia — Formal recognition of WBF’s regional role

    One year after Poznań, WBF was formally acknowledged in the Chair’s Conclusions of the Sofia Summit, held on 9–10 November 2020 under the joint Bulgarian and North Macedonian chairmanship of the Berlin Process.

    In addressing the future of the Process, the Joint Chairmanship stated:

    “The Joint Chairmanship commends the vision of Chancellor Merkel and the German stewardship of this process. As a multi-stakeholder initiative, it brings together different interested parties adding quality to the regional cooperation. This approach is much needed, especially in the context of COVID-19 and its long-term repercussions over the regional economies. The Joint Chairmanship also underscores the role of the EU/EC, as well as the regional organisations, such as RCC, CEFTA, TC, RYCO, WBF and others for their contribution to this process and moreover to the fulfilment of the overall mission and priorities of the Berlin Process.”

    Chair’s Conclusions · Sofia Summit, November 2020

    This acknowledgement carried particular significance for the Fund. For the first time in the conclusions of a Berlin Process Leaders’ Summit, the Fund was explicitly recognised among the regional organisations contributing to the fulfilment of the Berlin Process mission and priorities, alongside established regional actors such as the Regional Cooperation Council, CEFTA, the Transport Community, and RYCO.

    Western Balkans Summit of the Berlin Process — Sofia, 9–10 November 2020.

  3. 03
    2019

    Poznań — WBF joins the Berlin Process activities

    The earliest of the three milestones came under the Polish Presidency of the Berlin Process.

    On 5 July 2019, Poznań hosted the sixth Western Balkans Summit of the Berlin Process. For the first time, the Western Balkans Fund was invited to participate in Berlin Process activities, recognising the value of its contribution to regional cooperation, reconciliation, and people-to-people contacts. WBF also had its own exhibition booth at the accompanying fair, presenting its mission, grant-making activities, and regional impact.

    Sixth Western Balkans Summit of the Berlin Process — Poznań, 5 July 2019.

Partners

Working alongside the region's key institutions

The Fund's growth has been made possible by a widening circle of trusted partners and donors who share its commitment to a stable, connected and prosperous Western Balkans.

The European Union is the Fund's principal partner, co-funding its flagship grant schemes since 2021 through the Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance (IPA) under a dedicated EU–WBF Joint Action.

The Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation has supported the Fund's institutional strengthening and portfolio diversification since 2021, with the partnership renewed for the period 2026–2029.

The Government of Japan, through its Ministry of Foreign Affairs, has been a key partner in the Fund's advocacy agenda, enabling its flagship regional conferences on issues such as hate speech, women's leadership and disinformation. The International Visegrad Fund supports the Fund's WB6-V4 Fellowship and contributes to its broader institutional work, including the internship programme. In 2026 the Federal Republic of Germany signed its first Cooperation Agreement with the Fund. The Open Society Foundations – Western Balkans (OSF-WB) supported the WBF Regional Academy.

Beyond its donors, the Fund works closely with regional and international institutions, including the Regional Cooperation Council, the Regional Youth Cooperation Office and others, to enhance the visibility, effectiveness and impact of regional cooperation. These partnerships are built on the principles of complementarity, transparency, responsiveness and mutual respect.
